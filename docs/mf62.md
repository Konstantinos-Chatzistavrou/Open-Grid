# MF 5.2 / 6.1 / 6.2 tyre evaluator (`rs::mf62`)

`core/include/rs/mf62.hpp` + `core/src/mf62.cpp`: the complete steady-state Pacejka Magic Formula, version 5.2 (and PAC2002), 6.1 and 6.2, with a reader for
real tyre property files (`.tir`, FITTYP 5 / 6 / 21 / 61 / 62). One call gives all 30 MFeval outputs: Fx, Fy, Mx, My, Mz, effective and loaded radius, deflection,
contact patch, pneumatic trail, friction coefficients, stiffnesses and relaxation lengths, with combined slip, camber, inflation pressure, low speed and (optionally) turn slip.

## What the golden test caught

The first comparison, on MFeval's example tyres, passed everywhere. The golden test on the committed tyres then failed on exactly the 17 turn-slip points, in `Mz` and `Mzr` only. Interrogating the real `calculateMz0`
under Octave showed every intermediate equal except `Cr = zeta7 = (2/pi) acos(Mzp90 / |Drp|)`: when that ratio is beyond +-1, MATLAB's `acos` is **complex**, `cos(Cr x)` is evaluated with the complex `Cr`, and only
the final result goes through `real()`. The first version clamped the ratio (and a code comment claimed that gives the same real part, which is false). Now `Re cos(a + ib) = cos a cosh b` is evaluated exactly. The example tyres
never reached that branch, which is why a second, differently-built tyre set is worth having.

## What the comparison does and does not show

It shows that `mf62.cpp` is a faithful port of MFeval's `Solver.m` for those three tyres and 393 points each, including the low-speed, reverse, no-load, out-of-range, alpha*, limits-off and turn-slip branches.
It does **not** show that MFeval equals the commercial TNO solver (MFeval was matched to MF-Tyre 6.1.2.1 by its author; we have no TNO output), nor that the MF 6.2 radius model is right for a race tyre. Limits:
MFeval ran under Octave, not MATLAB (same maths, a different interpreter); six tyres in all (three local, three committed) do not exercise every coefficient; the two synthetic tyres have plausible, not measured, 6.x coefficients.

Two things MFeval did that a reader of its output should know:

- **Its rolling-radius loop is batch-dependent.** `while abs(Re_old - Re) > 1e-9` on a vector runs only while *all* rows are unconverged, so it stops as soon as any row converges (a standstill or no-load row does at once) and leaves
  the others at iteration 2: Re, omega, Rl, rho and Cz of a row then depend on which other rows share the call (omega off by up to 3e-5 relative in our first, batched run). The reference therefore evaluates one point per call.
- On the MF 5.2 example file (no turn-slip aligning coefficients) MFeval returns `-Inf` for Mz and Mzr at every turn-slip point (`CDrp = 0` gives `sin(0)` in a denominator); those 17 points are skipped by the test.

## Run MFeval and compare (the validation step)

MFeval is MATLAB code; it needs MATLAB or GNU Octave (Octave 8.4 worked: `sudo apt install octave`). The same script works in both; under Octave it calls the `+mfeval` package's `Solver` directly, because Octave resolves
`mfeval.readTIR` to the function `mfeval.m` instead of the package (the vendored files are not modified).

```bash
# Octave (Linux Mint / Ubuntu): sudo apt install octave
mkdir -p /tmp/mf62_ref
for v in 52 61 62; do
  octave --no-gui --eval "addpath('tools/validation'); mfeval_reference('$HOME/mfeval_ref/fsroot/doc/examples/MagicFormula${v}_Parameters.tir', 'data/validation/mf62/mf62_cases.csv', '/tmp/mf62_ref/mf${v}_mfeval.csv')"
done
cmake --build core/build --target rs_tests
RS_MF62_FIXTURES=$HOME/mfeval_ref/fsroot/doc/examples RS_MF62_REFERENCE=/tmp/mf62_ref core/build/rs_tests mf62_vs_mfeval
```

`~/mfeval_ref` is the unpacked `mfeval-4.3.1.mltbx` (the example `.tir` files are not in the repo: their data origin is not stated). The committed golden vectors are regenerated the same way:
`node tools/validation/make_mf6x_tyres.mjs`, then `mfeval_reference('data/tires/audi_245_40R18_pac2002.tir', 'data/validation/mf62/mf62_cases.csv', 'data/validation/mf62/golden/audi_pac2002_mfeval.csv', 21)` (the 4th argument supplies the
`FITTYP` the Chrono file lacks) and the same call without it for `data/validation/mf62/tyres/opengrid_mf61_synthetic.tir` / `..._mf62_synthetic.tir`. In MATLAB run the same
`mfeval_reference(...)` call; `mfeval` itself is on the path from `tools/validation/mfeval/` (the script adds it). The case list (`data/validation/mf62/mf62_cases.csv`,
393 points from `tools/validation/mf62_cases.mjs`) covers pure and combined slip, load, camber, pressure, low speed and reverse, alpha*, turn slip, limits off,
no load / below FZMIN / above FZMAX, and slips beyond the file's limits. The comparison needs 1e-6 relative agreement on every column except `instKya`
(it depends on how rows are grouped into a call); MFeval's own NaN results are skipped and counted.

## Where this follows MFeval and where it deviates

It follows MFeval's stage order and every correction MFeval made to match the TNO MF-Tyre 6.1.2.1 solver instead of the book: Bt from the paper (A58), Dt without
abs() on QDZ3 (A60), the equivalent slip angles (A54 / A55), `s` with Fz0 (A56), `Mx` and `My` (A48) in the TNO / paper form, `t` multiplied by LFZO, the low-load
corrections of Mx and My, the low-speed model, the limits (which also gate the "E <= 1" clips), and MF 6.2's loaded radius solved by the secant method.

Deliberate **deviations**, all for inputs where MFeval returns NaN or complex numbers (marked `DEVIATION` in `mf62.cpp`):

- turn slip with exactly `phit = 0` gives `acos(0/0)` in MFeval, hence NaN for Mz: here zeta7 = 1;
- turn slip at exactly `Vx = 0`: MFeval's `sign(0) = 0` makes `BHyp = Inf` and Fy NaN: here the sign is +1, as MFeval itself does for its `Vc_prime`;
- turn slip with no load: `BDrp = 0/0`: here the spin torque is 0;
- MF 5.2 / 6.1 deflection with `Q_FZ2 = 0` (no quadratic term) is solved linearly, the secant solver stops on a zero slope, and the iteration for omega is capped at 100 steps;
- FITTYP 5 is accepted as the MF 5.2 / PAC2002 family (the four FEDA truck tyres declare it); MFeval accepts 6, 21, 61 and 62 only.

## What the original source showed (the Julia port differs)

MFeval.jl (MIT) was the first cross-reference, but `Solver.m` differs from it in places, and `mf62.cpp` follows `Solver.m`: `phit * cos(alpha)` is applied first with the original
alpha; the pressure used for the rolling-radius estimate is the limited one; output column 10 is the processed `phit`; the output `muy` is forced positive when the limits are on;
`Fz_lowLimit` applies FZMIN even at zero load (the aligning moment then zeroes it); the "E <= 1" clips run only with the limit checks on; `LKYC` multiplies the whole MF 5.2 camber
stiffness; the turn-slip aligning moment has real `zeta7` / `zeta8` terms (MFeval.jl has placeholders); and the "Fy0 at gamma = 0" inside Mz keeps turn slip on.

## How this compares with the game's current tyre (`rs::magic_formula`)

Read against the MFeval source, the game's tyre is the MF 5.2 pure-slip equations with parts of the 6.x terms, its own combined-slip weighting, and its own contact-patch / thermal / carcass models.
Differences that matter for anyone calling it "MF 6.2" (each checked in `core/src/tire.cpp`):

- the "MF 6.2 hybrid" flag adds a hand-tuned `0.022 * gamma` camber shift to the kappa-induced weighting `Gyk` (`shyk`): not a Magic Formula term;
- after the weighting functions (which are normalised by `Gxa0` / `Gyk0`, as in MF 6.x) a friction-ellipse clamp trims Fx and Fy; the Magic Formula has no such step;
- `Mx` has 4 of its 14 `qSx` terms (`qSx1..3`, `qSx13`, plus `pPmx1`);
- the "MF 6.2 loaded radius" (`effective_rolling_radius_mf62`) is a hand-tuned camber term (0.042 / 0.011) and a force term; the real MF 6.2 radius is the secant-solved `Q_CAM1..3`, `Q_FYS1..3`, `Q_FCY2`
  relation, and the game's tyre has none of those coefficients;
- the aligning moment is close to the MF 6.x form but differs in details (the pressure term has the opposite sign convention, `Fz0` instead of `Fz0'`, no `LTR` and no `t * LFZO`);
- `pEy5` is part of the plain MF 6.1 `Ey`, not a 6.2 extension.

None of this says the game's tyres feel wrong: peak grip is a per-car calibration (docs/physics.md section 0). It says the claim "full MF 6.2" cannot be made for the runtime tyre, and that `rs::mf62` is the way to a real one.
